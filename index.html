About

Athena Saghi is a Human-Centered AI Researcher working where cognitive science, artificial intelligence and human–computer interaction (HCI) meet. She studies what happens to people’s judgment, creativity and biases when they work with large language models. She also builds systems that read a person’s cognitive state, from think-aloud speech to EEG, and adapt to it in real time.

She is a PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Southern California, in the Adaptive Computing (ACE) Lab, advised by Prof. Souti Chattopadhyay.

Research Approach

three questions
001

How people work with AI

I watch people do real work with AI, such as programming, writing and reading research, and measure what changes in how they reason. Mixed-methods studies show where trust, bias and habit enter the loop, and turn that into requirements for better tools.

Cognitive Biases in LLM-Assisted Software Development · ICSE 2026 ↗Beyond the Page · UIST 2025 ↗“Do as I say, not as I code?” · under reviewAuditing and Controlling AI Agent Actions in Spreadsheets · under review
  • Does working with AI amplify the cognitive biases people already have?
  • Why do people describe AI one way and rely on it in another?
002

How AI changes the mind

Working with AI does not only change the output. It changes the person producing it. I study that change at two levels: in behaviour, through the creative process, and in the brain, through EEG measures of connectivity and creative flow.

“Like Taking the Path of Least Resistance” · under revisionGenerative AI and Brain Connectivity · in submission
  • Does AI pull people toward the path of least resistance when they create?
  • Does working with generative AI change how the brain connects during creative work?
003

Aligning AI with human cognition

If AI changes how we think, it should also know how we are thinking. I build systems that sense a person’s state, through speech, physiology and behaviour, and decide when and how to step in, so the AI fits the person instead of the other way around.

Surfacing Latent Reasoning Gaps · under reviewNon-Markovian Modeling of EEG for Cognitive Fatigue · IEEE JBHI, under review
  • Can an AI sense a gap in someone’s reasoning and step in at the right moment?
  • Can a system read a person’s cognitive state, such as fatigue, engagement or flow, and adapt to it?
